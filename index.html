See how disputes work

If the CRA disagrees with you.

There is a defined path. Knowing the steps, and the deadlines, is half the battle.

Notice

The CRA issues an assessment or reassessment that you disagree with.

Objection

File a notice of objection, generally within 90 days of the notice of assessment. Individuals may have longer.

CRA review

An independent appeals officer at the CRA reviews the file and your submissions.

Tax Court

If still unresolved, you can appeal to the Tax Court of Canada, within its own deadline.

Fix it before they find it.

The CRA's Voluntary Disclosures Program lets taxpayers correct past errors or omissions. If accepted, it can reduce penalties and interest. It has conditions, and it only works if you come forward first.

Ask us if it applies
  • Voluntary: before the CRA starts enforcement
  • Complete: all relevant information disclosed
  • Involves a potential penalty
  • Includes payment of the tax owing

Masterclass

Assurance, explained.

Audit, review and compilation are three different promises about your financial statements. Here is how they differ, what an engagement involves, and how to choose.

Three levels of comfort

The more assurance a reader needs, the more work the accountant must do and document.

Level one
Compilation

No assurance. We present your information as financial statements. Standard: CSRS 4200.

Level two
Review

Limited assurance. Inquiry and analysis show whether anything suggests the statements need to change. Standard: CSRE 2400.

Level three
Audit

Reasonable assurance. Evidence is gathered and tested before we give an opinion. Standards: CAS.

Inside an audit

An audit follows a disciplined sequence, so the opinion rests on evidence.

Plan

We learn your business, agree the timetable and set materiality, the size of error that would matter to a reader.

Assess risk

We identify where the statements could be misstated and how your internal controls respond.

Test

We confirm balances with third parties, observe inventory, test transactions and examine supporting documents.

Conclude

We evaluate going concern and subsequent events, obtain management's representations and issue the report.

What the report can say

The auditor's opinion falls into one of four categories.

Unmodified

The statements present fairly, in all material respects. This is the outcome most clients receive.

Qualified

Fair, except for a specific matter that is material but not pervasive.

Adverse

The statements are materially and pervasively misstated.

Disclaimer

We could not obtain enough evidence to form an opinion.
